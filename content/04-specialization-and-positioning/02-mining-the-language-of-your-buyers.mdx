## The reason your content does not land

You probably do not have an ideas problem. You have a language problem.

Most creative people write in the language they speak at work. We use words like "positioning", "value proposition", "ecosystem", "framework". Our clients do not. They say things like "I feel invisible", "everything I post sounds fake", "my best clients keep ghosting me".

The first language is yours. The second language is theirs. The work of marketing is to write in the second one.

This page shows you how to find that language. You do not have to invent it. It already exists, in public, in massive volume. You just have to go get it.

## Diagnose before you prescribe

Doctors do not start with a prescription. They start with a question. Where does it hurt. When did it start. What have you tried.

Marketing works the same way. If you can name what is wrong with enough specificity that the reader feels caught, you have earned the right to offer a fix. If you cannot, you sound like every other person selling something.

The order matters:

1. Find the pain in their words.
2. Show them you see it clearly.
3. Then, and only then, point at the door.

Skip step one and the rest does not work. You will be selling a solution to a problem nobody admits they have.

> **Tip:** When you describe their pain, do not also describe how to fix it. The fix is what they pay you for. If you give it away in the post, they will save the post and never come back.

## Where to find the actual words

The internet is one giant focus group. Your buyers are already complaining about their problem in public. Your job is to listen, not to make things up.

Places to look:

- Reddit threads in your buyers' niche. Search for "frustrated", "stuck", "tried", "wasted".
- One-star and three-star reviews of competing products or courses. The three-star reviews are gold. They tell you what almost worked.
- Comments under popular posts in your space. Skip the praise. Read the disagreements.
- Replies to AI-generated content in your niche. People love to point out what feels fake.
- Quora and old forum threads. They rank for years and capture honest questions.

You are looking for two things. Repeating phrases. And emotional words. Words like ashamed, exhausted, fake, invisible, stuck.

When you see the same complaint show up in four different places, in similar words, you have found a pattern. That pattern is your next post.

## Use AI to lurk at scale

Reading forums for three months is a great use of time, if you have it. Most people do not. AI can do the lurking part for you.

Try this prompt structure with any large language model. The exact words can change, but the shape stays the same.

```text
Act as a market researcher who specializes in raw, unfiltered audience pain.

You spent three months lurking in online communities for {your niche}. You read every angry Reddit post, every bitter comment on {primary platform}, every low-star review of {two or three competing products or courses}.

Pull out:
- The 10 most repeated complaints, in their own words.
- A table with three columns: what they tried, what happened, what they felt.
- The 3 emotional words that show up over and over.

Cite the source for each quote. If you cannot cite a source, mark it as illustrative.

Before you start, ask me any clarifying questions you need.
```

Tell it the niche. Tell it the competing products by name. Tell it where the conversations live. Then read the output the way a journalist reads a transcript. Underline the lines that feel real. Discard the rest.

> **Warning:** AI will sometimes invent quotes that sound plausible. Always ask it to cite sources. If a quote does not have a link, treat it as a hypothesis, not a fact. Search a snippet of it on the web. If nothing comes back, drop it.

## Turn raw quotes into content hooks

Once you have the language, the content writes itself. You do not need fresh ideas. You need to translate.

**Example.** A real complaint from a creator in my notes: "I did everything they told me to do, and somewhere in the process I stopped sounding like me."

That single line gives you, easily, a week of content:

- A LinkedIn post that opens with the quote and then asks the reader if they have felt the same.
- A short video where you read the quote and explain why it happens.
- A carousel titled "Conversion-first vs identity-first content. One drains you. One builds you."
- A newsletter section called "the moment you stopped sounding like you".
- A workshop hook: "I paid a ghostwriter and lost my voice. Here is what I learned."

Notice that none of these are clever. They are not trying to be smart. They are using the same words the reader already says in their own head. That is the whole trick.

## A simple table that does most of the work

A format that earns its weight is a three-column table for each pain pattern.

| What they tried | What happened | What they felt |
|----|----|----|
| Hired a ghostwriter | Posts went up, engagement dropped | Like a stranger in their own feed |
| Used AI for everything | Output was fast and polished | Disconnected, like they were disappearing |
| Followed a guru's playbook | Got some likes, no clients | Confused, ashamed of admitting it did not work |

Build this table for your niche. Three rows is enough to start. Each row is a future post. Each row is also a future objection you can address in your sales conversations.

## Try This

Pick one buyer you know well. A past client, a friend in your audience, someone whose problem you understand.

1. Open any large language model.
2. Paste the prompt from the section above. Fill in the niche and the competing products.
3. Run it. Take the top three quotes that ring true.
4. For each quote, write one social post that does only this: name the pain, agree it hurts, stop. Do not solve it.
5. Post one. See what happens in the comments.

You are not testing the quote. You are testing whether your audience recognizes itself in their own words.

## Summary

People will not buy from you until they feel understood. Feeling understood comes from hearing their own words read back to them. You do not need to make those words up. They are sitting in public, in forums and reviews, waiting to be collected. AI lets you do the collecting in an afternoon instead of three months. Once you have the language, the hardest part of content stops being "what to post" and becomes "which pain to name today".
