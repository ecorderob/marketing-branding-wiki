## Two kinds of unhappy

Most ambitious creatives carry around a low-grade unhappiness with their work.

That unhappiness is not the problem. It is the engine. It is what keeps you reading, learning, redrawing, redoing the deck, redesigning the website. People who are perfectly happy with their current work usually stop growing.

The problem is where you point the unhappiness. If you point it at your skills and your plans, it becomes fuel. If you point it at yourself as a person, it becomes a slow form of self-attack that quietly drains you.

This page is the small move that changes which one happens.

## The wrong target

Most self-criticism sounds like this in the head.

"I am not good enough."

"I am behind where I should be."

"There is something wrong with me. Other people figured this out years ago."

Notice that all three of these sentences are about you, the person. Not about a skill. Not about a plan. Just a verdict on the whole human.

When you talk to yourself this way, two things happen. First, there is no useful action that follows. You cannot fix "I am not good enough" the way you can fix "my pricing page is not converting." Second, the part of you that is supposed to do the actual work feels attacked, and the work gets harder, not easier.

> **Warning:** Calling yourself bad is not the same as having high standards. People with high standards know where the standard sits and what step gets them closer to it. People being self-critical only know that they have failed an unstated test.

## The right target

The same energy works much better when you redirect it from the person to the skill or the plan.

"I am not good enough at running discovery calls yet."

"My current pricing structure is leaving money on the table."

"I have not built the portfolio piece that would attract the clients I want."

These sentences sound less dramatic. They are also useful. Each of them comes with a next move attached.

The rule is small but it carries a lot of weight.

- Do not be unhappy with who you are. Be unhappy with what you can currently do.
- Do not be unhappy with your situation. Be unhappy with your current plan.

When you make this swap, the same energy starts pulling you forward instead of pulling you down.

## How to swap mid-thought

You will not always catch the bad target before the thought lands. That is fine. You can swap it after.

The mental move has two steps.

Step one. When you notice a "I am bad" thought, pause and ask: "Am I unhappy with who I am, or with what I can currently do?"

Step two. Rewrite the thought as a sentence about a skill or a plan. Keep the energy. Move the target.

**Example.**

Original: "I am terrible at sales. I am just not built for it."

Swap: "I am not good at the first ten minutes of a sales call yet. I have never had a real script for that part."

The first sentence ends the conversation. The second one starts a new project. Same feeling, different output.

## Resentment is also a signal

There is a related feeling that uses the same logic. Resentment.

When you feel resentment toward your work, your client list, or your business, it is rarely random. Resentment usually shows up when something you are doing is not aligned with who you want to be. The work pays well, but it bores you. The client respects you, but you do not respect their goals. The business runs, but it runs you.

The instinct is to push the resentment down or to call yourself ungrateful. Neither helps. The resentment is information.

The same swap works.

Bad target: "What is wrong with me, I should be grateful, why am I like this?"

Good target: "Which specific part of this work or this client mix is creating the resentment, and what would the next plan need to look like to remove it?"

You may not be able to fix it tomorrow. Naming the source moves it from a vague heaviness to a concrete problem with edges.

> **Tip:** A simple way to find the source: list your last five projects. Mark each as "want more of this," "fine," or "want less of this." The pattern is usually clear within five entries.

## The "what would I tell a friend" check

When you are stuck pointing the unhappiness at yourself, the fastest unstick is to ask: what would I say to a friend in the same situation?

You would almost never tell a friend "you are not good enough." You would tell them "you have not had a chance to practice that yet" or "your current plan is not designed for what you actually want." Talk to yourself the way you would talk to them.

This is not about being soft. It is about being accurate. The friend version is also the more useful version.

## Try this

Open a notes file. Write down the last three "I am bad at X" thoughts you remember having this month. Be specific. Pricing, sales calls, design, writing, time, anything.

For each one, rewrite it as a sentence about a specific skill or plan. Then write one small action you can take in the next two weeks to move that skill or plan one step forward. Not the whole solution. Just the next step.

When the old thought shows up again, replace it with the new sentence and the next step. Keep the file. Add to it.

## Summary

Dissatisfaction is normal and useful. It only becomes destructive when you aim it at yourself as a person instead of at your current skills or plan. Watch your inner sentences. If they end the conversation, rewrite them so they start a project. Resentment works the same way. It is data about what is misaligned, not proof that something is wrong with you. Move the target, keep the energy.
